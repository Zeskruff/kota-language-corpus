# kota-language-corpus

## Project Overview
  The Kota Language Documentation Corpus is an independent language documentation project focused on the Dravidian language Kota and the communities that speak it in the __Nilgiri Hills__ of __Tamil Nadu, India__. The project began with fieldwork conducted in __August 2026__, with the goal of creating an organized collection of naturally occurring Kota speech alongside transcriptions, translations, recordings, annotations, photos, and field notes from my time there.
  The project grew from my interest in linguistics and in the relationships between language, culture, and community. During fieldwork, I worked with Kota community members to record conversations and interviews covering topics such as childhood, family life, music, traditions, religion, pottery, social structure, marriage, and language use. Interviews were conducted with the assistance of Tamil speaking family members and the help of a guide from the __Nilgiri Adivasi Welfare Association__ (NAWA), whose help made this entire project possible. Speakers provided responses in Kota and, where possible, translated their responses into Tamil. 
  The corpus is organized around individual recording sessions. Each session contains the original audio recording, metadata and accompanying field notes. They also may contain a Kota transcription, a Tamil translation, an English translation, and ELAN annotations. Metadata documents information such as the date and location of a session, participants, topics discussed, languages used, and relevant recording information. Field notes provide additional context about the circumstances of each recording and observations made during fieldwork.
  This project is intended as both a record of my fieldwork and as an introduction to the process of language documentation. Since this is a small scale independent project conducted over a limited period of time, the corpus is __NOT__ intended to represent the full range of Kota language or community life. Transcriptions and translations are also subject to revision as my understanding of the language develops.
  This repository will continue to develop as recordings are transcribed, annotated, organized, and documented

## Consent and Access
  Before each recording, participants were verbally informed that the conversation would be recorded for a language documentation project and were asked whether they agreed to participate and whether the resulting materials could be shared publicly. Participants gave verbal permission before recording.
  Users of this corpus should not redistribute restricted materials or use recordings, transcripts, photographs, or other materials in ways that conflict with the permissions under which they were collected.
  
## How to Use
  The corpus is organized by recording session and can be explored through the folders provided in this repository.

Recordings: Original audio recordings from fieldwork. Access may be restricted depending on participant consent.
Transcripts: Kota transcriptions, Tamil transcriptions, and English translations of recorded speech.
ELAN: .eaf annotation files linking transcriptions and translations to specific portions of the recordings.
Metadata: Information about each recording session, including date, location, participants, topic, languages, and recording details.
Field Notes: Notes documenting the circumstances of each recording and observations made during fieldwork.
Photos: Selected photographs providing additional context about the fieldwork and communities visited.
Report: Documentation of the project's background, methodology, findings, and limitations.

  For individual recordings, the corresponding transcript, translation, ELAN annotation, metadata, and field notes should be identified using the same session ID. For example, files beginning with __K01__ correspond to the same recording session.
